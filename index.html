Purpose

The Museum’s purpose

The Museum brings together Palo Alto’s stories across housing, medicine, art, education, technology and civic life.

It examines how people changed the place, including the choices they faced and what helped or hindered them.

It also brings people together through exhibitions, conversations, classes and hands-on activities, with opportunities to contribute their own stories and ideas.

Sources: Board presentation, pp. 14–16, 23 · Field Notes, pp. 4, 8–9
Sources and notes
Board presentation, p. 23
The Palo Alto Museum connects Palo Alto’s stories across fields. It explores the choices and conditions behind past changes, brings people together around today’s ideas and questions, and creates opportunities to contribute to what happens next.

Page 14 identifies people as the subject, change as the phenomenon and how change happens as the lens. Page 16 lists exhibitions, community conversations, classes, performances and hands-on activities. Field Notes page 8 describes residents adding stories and contributors imagining Palo Alto in 2044.

Mission

How the mission wording developed

Board version

The Palo Alto Museum explores how change happens in Palo Alto and brings people together to learn, connect, (create) and play a part in what comes next.

Subsequent working version

The Palo Alto Museum explores how change happens in Palo Alto and brings people together to explore, connect, and create what comes next.

Marguerite’s latest thought

The Palo Alto Museum tells the human story of change from around the block to around the world and brings people together to explore, connect, and create what comes next.

Marguerite’s version puts people first with “the human story” and avoids repeating “explore.” It also replaces “how change happens in Palo Alto” with “from around the block to around the world,” which widens the scope but drops the Museum’s lens and its place. The proposed synthesis on the next slide keeps her human focus and restores both.

Mission

The proposed mission

Marguerite’s latest thought

The Palo Alto Museum tells the human story of change from around the block to around the world how change happens in Palo Alto and brings people together to explore, connect, and create what comes next.

→ Next: the proposed change

“How change happens in Palo Alto”

“From around the block to around the world” widens the scope but drops the Museum’s lens and its place. The proposal keeps Marguerite’s “human story” and restores both: people are the subject, how change happens is the lens, and Palo Alto is where we look.

“Tells” instead of “explores”

“Tells” is clearer and more confident. The cost is that it can suggest the Museum speaks and visitors listen. Marguerite has argued for “explores” because the story is not finished and the word gives participants agency. In this version, “explore” moves to the visitors in the second half of the sentence.

“Create”

The mission keeps “create.” Marguerite has argued that it is broader than making: dialogue, discussion, community art, business plans. The working version and Marguerite’s version both use it.

Sources: Board presentation, pp. 14, 16, 24 · Marguerite’s comments · Field Notes, p. 8
Sources and notes

The mission is a proposal. The only change from Marguerite’s version is the phrase “how change happens in Palo Alto,” which comes from the Board version. The choice between “tells” and “explores” is open and appears in the decisions on the last slide.

Marguerite’s arguments for “explores” and for a broad reading of “create” come from her comments on the mission wording. Page 16 of the board presentation and Field Notes page 8 describe the range of things people make and contribute: art, writing, stories and hands-on work.

The proposed mission is 27 words, the same length as the Board version.

Vision

The proposed vision

Current working vision

More people in more places understand how the world around them came to be, how people of many backgrounds shape it together, and how they can take part what part they can play in what comes next.

Current working vision: More people in more places understand how the world around them came to be, how people of many backgrounds shape it together, and how they can take part in what comes next.

→ Next: the proposed change

Past

how the world around them came to be

Present

how people of many backgrounds shape it together

Future

what part they can play in what comes next

The vision stays one sentence, and one phrase changes. “How they can take part” is about method; “what part they can play” is about each person’s role. The new phrase also uses the Board’s own words: “People can play a part in what happens next.” The three clauses now run in order, from past to present to future.

Sources: Board presentation, pp. 13, 18–19, 24

Tagline

What we want the tagline to do

Help people understand and remember what the Museum stands for.

The tagline appears alongside “Palo Alto Museum” and the mission and vision. It does not have to say something new. It can compress, dramatize, reframe or invite. We compare the four options on four questions.

Strategic connection

Does it express something fundamental to the Museum?

Contribution

What does it do that the mission and vision cannot do as well: compress, provoke, invite, dramatize or reframe?

Standalone meaning

What does someone reasonably understand without our explanation?

Trade-off

What important idea does it leave implicit, and what might it wrongly suggest?

Sources: Board presentation, pp. 14, 18–19 · Earlier discussion draft
Sources and notes

All four lines draw on the Board’s own materials, so each passes the first question in some form. The comparison turns on the other three.

Understanding and memory are goals, not outcomes established by these materials. No audience test is included.

Tagline option A

Palo Alto Museum

Nothing
is inevitable.

What it does

Turns the Museum’s method into a worldview. The Field Notes start each story while people were still making choices and nobody knew what would work, then carry that into the present: what happens next is not fixed either.

Read on its own

A claim about possibility. Without the Museum’s stories beside it, it could read as a general statement about life.

Trade-off

It leaves people unnamed and makes a broader claim than the historical examples. The Field Notes wording, “Nothing here was inevitable,” ties it to the place.

Sources: Field Notes, p. 7 · Board presentation, pp. 10–14, 19
Sources and notes
Field Notes, p. 7
Nothing here was inevitable. What happens next isn’t either.
Field Notes, p. 7
Most stories look obvious once you know the ending. The Museum starts earlier, when people were still making choices and nobody knew what would work.
Tagline option B

Palo Alto Museum

People
change things.

What it does

Compresses the Museum’s human-centered account of change into three words. It is close to a public form of the mission’s “human story of how change happens.”

Read on its own

Clear and direct. “Things” is deliberately open, so the line says little about what visitors will see or do.

Trade-off

It can overstate individual agency unless the Museum shows the conditions and collaborators behind each change. It does not express the interest in how change happens. The exact phrase is already in use elsewhere.

Sources: Field Notes, pp. 3, 7 · Board presentation, pp. 13–14
Sources and notes
Field Notes, p. 3
It’s about Palo Alto and the people who keep changing it. Some of it is history, some of it is what’s happening now, and some of it is about what comes next.

Board presentation page 13 gives examples: someone saved photographs, returned to a meeting, painted a clinic wall, or asked what an empty building could become.

Existing use of the exact phrase was raised in review. Check the extent of that use before adopting the line.

Tagline option C

Palo Alto Museum

Change
something.

What it does

Turns participation from a description into an invitation. Residents add stories to I AM PALO ALTO, and contributors imagine the city in Palo Alto 2044. “Something” leaves room for a small contribution.

Read on its own

An instruction. It does not say what to change or why.

Trade-off

It may feel demanding to someone who came to learn, see an exhibition or spend time with others.

Sources: Field Notes, p. 8 · Board presentation, pp. 18–19
Sources and notes
Board presentation, p. 18
learn, make, connect, and play a part in what happens next

The concern that the line may feel demanding is our judgment, not an observed visitor reaction.

Tagline option D

Palo Alto Museum

Change doesn’t
just happen.

What it does

Turns the Museum’s interpretive lens into a provocative proposition. The board presentation follows each change through questions: what existed, who wanted something different, what they did, and what helped or blocked them.

Read on its own

It promises an explanation and invites the obvious question: then how does it happen?

Trade-off

It does not name the people involved and says less about the visitor’s own part.

Sources: Board presentation, pp. 8–9, 14 · Field Notes, p. 7

Discussion

Decisions for discussion

Mission

Does the proposed mission describe the Museum’s work accurately? Do we accept “tells” in place of “explores”?

Vision

Should the vision end with “what part they can play in what comes next” in place of “how they can take part”?

Tagline

Which job should the tagline do: introduce possibility, compress the mission, invite people to take part, or make them ask how change happens?

Sources and notes

The mission and vision are open wording decisions. The tagline comparison does not recommend a line. Once the Board chooses a direction, the next step is to develop that line and try it with a small group of visitors.
